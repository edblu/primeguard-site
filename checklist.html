1. King Tide Prep (Oct 24-30, peak Oct 26-28)

  • Clear street drains, swales, and gutters of leaves and debris
  • Move patio items, bins, and anything that floats out of low spots
  • Trim low branches and pick up yard debris before water rises
  • Note where water pools in the yard (those spots need attention after)
  • Rinse salt water off plants and lawn edges after the tide drops
  • Watch for fungus and dead patches in the two weeks after

2. Snowbird Make-Ready (Oct-Nov arrivals)

  • Full lawn cut, edge, and trim before arrival day
  • Clear overgrowth from walkways, driveway, and pool deck
  • Check irrigation is running and heads are not broken
  • Pressure wash the driveway and entry if they have gone green
  • Junk and debris haul-away from the yard and garage entry
  • Set a no-contract maintenance schedule so the home stays handled all season

3. HOA Lawn Rules That Catch People

  • Grass height limits (many HOAs cite you before you notice)
  • Weeds in beds and sidewalk cracks count as violations in most communities
  • No debris, bins, or equipment stored in view of the street
  • Sidewalk and driveway edges must stay clean and trimmed
  • Dead patches and bare dirt areas can trigger letters too
  • A photo of your yard after each visit is your proof of care if a letter arrives
